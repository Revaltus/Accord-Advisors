## Challenges churches face that call for an experienced CPA firm

Churches handle money differently than businesses do, and the gaps show up quickly without specialized help. These are the issues we see most often:

- **Mixed funds:** Building campaigns, missions gifts, and general giving end up in one generic chart of accounts, so restricted dollars blur with unrestricted ones.
- **Volunteer-run books:** A treasurer or committee member carries the bookkeeping on top of a full-time job, and knowledge leaves when they do.
- **Clergy compensation rules:** Housing allowances and dual tax status are easy to get wrong, and mistakes can mean IRS penalties.
- **Thin internal controls:** Offering counts, deposits, and disbursement approvals often lack separation of duties.
- **Late or confusing reports:** Boards and elders get numbers too slowly, or in a format only an accountant can read.
- **Filing and audit pressure:** Nonprofit filings, denominational reporting, and annual audits arrive with little room for error.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Fund accounting for churches

**Q: What is fund accounting for churches?**
A: Fund accounting tracks each designated pool of money, like a building fund or mission fund, separately from general operating income. It shows whether restricted gifts are being spent as donors intended, giving church boards and treasurers a clear picture of each fund's balance and activity every month.

**Q: Does Accord Advisors work with churches outside Bloomington?**
A: Yes. Accord Advisors serves churches and religious organizations nationwide. The firm is well-known in the greater Indianapolis area with offices in Bloomington and Avon, Indiana. Working through a secure online platform so finance committees and treasurers can access reports and collaborate remotely, Accord Advisors serves churches across the United States.

**Q: How does clergy pay affect church payroll?**
A: Ministers typically have dual tax status: employees for income tax purposes but self-employed for Social Security and Medicare. Housing allowances must be designated in advance and documented correctly. Getting this wrong risks IRS penalties, which is why payroll setup for clergy needs specific attention.

**Q: What does a fixed monthly fee include for church accounting?**
A: A fixed monthly fee covers ongoing bookkeeping, fund reconciliations, financial statement preparation, and platform access, so a church budgets a predictable cost instead of facing variable hourly invoices during busy seasons like year-end giving or audit prep.

**Q: Do small or new churches need Business Foundation Services?**
A: Yes. New or growing churches often start with a chart of accounts that wasn't built for fund tracking. Setting up proper fund structures and internal controls early prevents misallocated gifts, weak cash handling controls, and costly corrections during future audits.
